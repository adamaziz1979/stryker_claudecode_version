Executive Summary
Stryker is a $25bn medtech business that has used acquisitions to shift towards higher-growth segments like peripheral vascular, robotics and connected care. 2026 has shown the cost of that complexity: a global cyberattack in March and a peripheral vascular supply backlog that has cut about 26% from its market value over the year. The ANZ Data & Analytics team is hiring a strategic partner and a platform technical lead at the same time, while it moves to Microsoft Fabric and Databricks. The Senior Lead Data Analyst sits where those two meet. The job is to turn data into trusted, dollar-quantified decisions while the systems, structure and definitions underneath keep changing.

2. How the Company Became What It Is Today

1941 to 2012: Origins
Orthopaedic surgeon Homer Stryker founded the company in 1941. It grew into a decentralised, division-led device maker.
2013: Robotics
Mako Surgical ($1.65bn) brought robotic-assisted surgery into the portfolio and turned joint replacement into a technology platform.
2020 to 2024: Scale and software
Wright Medical (2020) added extremities scale. Vocera (2022) and care.ai (2024) moved Stryker into clinical communication, virtual care and ambient AI.
2025: Vascular and focus
Inari (about $4.9bn) created a peripheral vascular growth platform. The US spinal implants business was sold to VB Spine, and a new CFO was appointed.
2026: Execution under stress
A new COO and the Ortho Tech reorganisation arrived just before the March cyberattack. The Q2 rebound was followed by a peripheral vascular supply backlog that now runs into Q4. Inference Revenue has almost doubled since 2020, but so have the number of systems, acquired data estates and supply chains to integrate.

Stage 3: Top 5 Experiences to Lead With

The interviewers will probably want proof of three things: you can quantify dollar impact, you can make numbers trusted across the business, and you can run an analytics project from problem to deployment while lifting the team around you. Each of these five covers a different part of that.

1

Trusted KPIs across the business (Cisco, Data Solutions & Insights)

Why it matters here: The JD asks for governance "including documented definitions, calculations, sources, lineage" and for visualisation that helps leaders "quickly identify key insights". The Ortho Tech reorganisation and ERP change make shared definitions a live issue (Working Hypothesis).
STeams saw different numbers for the same KPI (1 to 5% gaps), which caused 3 to 5 escalations a month. TGet to zero inconsistency and restore confidence in the numbers. AMoved logic out of Power BI into shared SQL single-source views. Added catalogues with plain-English definitions and master dashboards per business area. Set up tripwire alerts for any variance over 2%. R27 KPIs went from 62% to 100% consistency. Dashboards cut from 70+ to 46. Escalations went from 3 to 5 a month to zero. Workspace health went from 60% to 93%.
"The first thing I usually fix is whether people trust the number. At Cisco, the same KPI was showing different values depending on which dashboard you opened."
Likely question: "Tell me about a time you improved trust in reporting." · Source: Resume (DS&I Lead); STAR "Trusted metrics and KPI Consistency"; portfolio case study 1.

2

ThoughtSpot commercial case and self-service rollout (Cisco, FY24 to FY26)

Why it matters here: The JD asks twice for financial acumen to "develop financial impacts… and evaluate new opportunities". Stryker ANZ is rolling out new platforms (Fabric, Databricks), and this shows you can make a platform decision pay and get people to use it.
SYou were paying internal cross-charges for a capped ThoughtSpot instance, and analysts were swamped with simple requests. TGet a direct instance at better value and move simple demand to self-service. AModelled the capacity options, negotiated directly with the vendor alongside other data leads, and built governed datasets and automated onboarding. RThe $70K / 250M-row option gave 2.9x better value, with 65% lower cost per million rows. Weekly users grew from 46 to 196 (+326%). Monthly queries went from 8.1K to 19.2K. Data requests fell 33% (about 738 hours and $68.9K a year).
"Here's one where I had to put a dollar value on a platform choice before anyone would fund it."

Performance-based laptop refresh with anomaly detection (Cisco, DS&I Lead)

Why it matters here: The JD wants experience leading end-to-end projects "from problem and requirements definition to code/model validation and deployment", and cross-functional work that draws on domain expertise. It fits Stryker's supply-and-demand forecasting problem in peripheral vascular (Working Hypothesis).
SLaptops were refreshed by age rather than need, so budget and supply didn't match what users needed. TRefresh based on evidence from device telemetry. AWeighed supervised ML, unsupervised ML, GenAI and statistical options against build effort, upkeep and reliability. Chose unsupervised anomaly detection. Improved stock forecasting. Supported millions in potential global asset savings.

Root cause of BSODs and app crashes (Cisco, Lead Analyst, 2017 to 2019)
